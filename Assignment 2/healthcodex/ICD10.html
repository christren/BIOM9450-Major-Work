ICD-10 and ICD-10-AM: Classifying Health Conditions

Plenary speaker: Dr Stephen Strange
Time: 09:00–09:45

What is ICD-10?

ICD-10 stands for the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems, Tenth Revision. It is a World Health Organization (WHO) classification that groups diseases and other health-related conditions into categories identified by codes containing letters and numbers.

Using a shared classification makes it much easier to organise health records and compare patterns of illness and death. Its categories cover more than named diseases: they also include symptoms, abnormal findings and external causes of injury or illness.

What is ICD-10-AM?

ICD-10-AM is the Australian Modification of ICD-10. It adapts the international classification for Australian requirements. Public and private hospitals in Australia utlise it to classify diseases, injuries and related health problems for episodes of admitted patient care.

How is it Used with ACHI and ACS?

ICD-10-AM is used alongside the Australian Classification of Health Interventions, known as ACHI, and the Australian Coding Standards, known as ACS. ACHI classifies interventions such as surgeries and therapies. ACS provides guidance to support consistent application of ICD-10-AM and ACHI.

These components have different roles. A patient's health condition is classified using ICD-10-AM, while an intervention may be classified using ACHI. Coders apply the relevant standards when translating documented clinical information into codes.

Classification Compared with Messaging

ICD-10-AM helps describe which category a health condition belongs to. It does not define how one computer system sends a patient record to another. Messaging standards such as those developed by HL7 address information exchange. Classification and messaging can therefore work together, but they are not interchangeable.
