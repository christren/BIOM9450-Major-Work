HL7: Exchanging Health Information

Plenary speaker: Dr Bruce Banner
Time: 13:00–13:45

What is HL7?

Health Level Seven International, abbreviated as HL7, develops standards for sharing electronic health information. Healthcare organisations often use differing computer systems, so they need joint ways to organise and exchange data. HL7 forms these standards and describes how information should be structured and communicated between these systems.

Why are messaging standards needed?

A hospital may use separate applications for patient registration, laboratory testing and clinical records. These applications need to exchange information without staff repetitively entering the same details. For example, a laboratory result needs to reach the clinical system and be associated with the correct patient. Agreed message structures help the receiving system interpret the information.

Different HL7 Standards

HL7 provides several approaches to exchanging information. HL7 Version 2 supports messaging between healthcare applications. Clinical Document Architecture, or CDA, defines the structure of clinical documents exchanged between systems.

Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources, or FHIR, is another HL7 standard. It organises information into reusable building blocks called resources. These can represent information such as patients and clinical observations. Applications can exchange these resources to support healthcare workflows.

Messaging and Medical Codes

Messaging standards and medical classification systems have different jobs. HL7 standards help structure and exchange information, while classification systems such as ICD-10-AM provide codes for diseases and related health problems. A system exchanging health information may therefore use both a messaging standard and coded clinical information.
